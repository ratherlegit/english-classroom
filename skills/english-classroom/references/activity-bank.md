# Activity Bank

Organized by skill focus. Pick activities that match the stated CEFR level (see cefr-levels.md) — scale complexity of language, not just the activity mechanic.

Prefer activities built from generated text (dialogues, stories, transcripts, gap-fills) over ones needing the tutor to source real external material. The **Listening** activities below are the main exception — dictogloss, gap-fill-on-transcript, and song-lyrics work from a real audio source; when picking one, write a script the tutor can read aloud instead of requiring a found recording wherever that's a workable substitute. See SKILL.md's "Choosing the activities" section for the cap on externally-sourced activities per lesson.

## Grammar
- **Warm-up**: error correction on the board (mix correct/incorrect sentences using yesterday's target), quick concept-check questions
- **Presentation**: timeline diagrams (for tenses), context-building mini-dialogues, guided discovery from a short text
- **Controlled practice**: gap-fill, sentence transformation, matching halves of sentences, ordering scrambled words
- **Freer practice**: personalization ("tell your partner three things you've never done"), info-gap (each student has different info, must ask questions using target structure), running dictation

## Vocabulary
- **Warm-up**: categorize words into groups, odd-one-out, quick pictionary/mime
- **Presentation**: realia/images, definitions matching, collocation grids
- **Practice**: word webs, fill-in-the-blank stories, categorize by connotation (positive/negative/neutral)
- **Production**: use 8-10 target words to tell a story in pairs, "just a minute" (talk on a topic using as many target words as possible)

## Speaking
- Role-play with cue cards, opinion line (agree/disagree, stand along a spectrum and justify), discussion circles/fishbowl, debate, problem-solving in groups (survival scenarios, ranking tasks), interview partner then report back to class

## Listening
- Gist-first listening (one simple question before detail questions), dictogloss (listen, reconstruct text from memory + notes in groups), listen-and-sequence images/events, gap-fill on a transcript excerpt, song lyrics gap-fill for lower levels

## Reading
- Jigsaw reading (different students read different sections, share info to complete a task), skim for gist then scan for detail, prediction from headline/image before reading, true/false/not-given for exam prep, vocabulary-in-context guessing before dictionary use

## Writing
- Model text analysis (highlight structure/linking words before students write their own), collaborative writing (build a story sentence by sentence), peer editing with a checklist, timed exam-style writing under real conditions, sentence-combining exercises for lower levels

## Warm-ups usable for any lesson
- Two truths and a lie, would-you-rather, quick survey (mingle and ask 3 classmates), word association chain, this-or-that voting

## Group classroom notes

Use these adjustments when an activity is run with a class instead of one student.

- Prefer formats that work in pairs and small groups. If an activity is a monologue or a one-to-one exchange, turn it into a pair task or a rotating group task.
- Give every activity a suggested grouping (mixed-level pairs, same-level trios, whole class) and a Support, Core, and Stretch version. See `differentiation.md`.
- Warm-ups work well as whole-class or pair activities. Choose ones every student can answer regardless of level.
- Limit activities where one student performs alone for the whole room, and rotate who goes first.

# CEFR Level Calibration

Use these to keep grammar complexity, vocabulary, and instruction language appropriate to the stated level.

## A1 (Beginner)
- Simple present, present continuous, basic imperatives, "there is/are", simple possessives
- High-frequency, concrete vocabulary (family, food, numbers, daily routine)
- Instructions: very short, simple sentences; rely on gesture/visuals; avoid idioms

## A2 (Elementary)
- Past simple, going to future, comparatives/superlatives, basic modals (can/must)
- Everyday topics: travel, shopping, past experiences
- Instructions: short sentences, can handle 2-step directions

## B1 (Intermediate)
- Present perfect, past continuous, first conditional, common phrasal verbs
- Can discuss opinions, plans, familiar abstract topics
- Instructions: multi-step directions fine; can handle some abstraction

## B2 (Upper Intermediate)
- Second/third conditionals, passive voice, reported speech, wider modal range
- Can discuss abstract topics, express nuanced opinions, follow extended discourse
- Suitable for most exam-prep contexts (IELTS 5.5-6.5 band range)

## C1 (Advanced)
- Full range of conditionals/modals, complex subordination, nuanced collocations
- Idiomatic language, register-shifting (formal/informal), inference-based reading/listening
- Exam-prep: IELTS 7+, Cambridge CAE range

## C2 (Mastery)
- Near-native range of structures; focus shifts to precision, style, and register
- Vocabulary work targets nuance/connotation over new items
- Lessons often skew toward authentic native materials (news, literature, unedited audio)

## Quick calibration check
When writing example sentences or instructions for a lesson, ask: would a learner at this exact level need to guess at unfamiliar structures to understand the *instructions themselves*? If yes, simplify — the metalanguage shouldn't be harder than the target language.

# Teacher's Proven Activities

This is the tutor's own list of activities that have worked well in practice. Prefer these over `activity-bank.md` whenever one fits the lesson — treat `activity-bank.md` as the fallback, not the other way around.

When picking "randomly," don't pick uniformly at random from the whole list — filter first to activities that fit the level, the time available, and the lesson's language/skill focus, then choose among those. When a specific activity is requested by name, use it as-is and adapt only the topic/content/scenario to the student.

**Default to self-contained activities** — ones built entirely from generated text (dialogues, stories, scenarios, discussion questions) that need nothing beyond the plan itself. Three activities on this list inherently need the tutor to source real external material (a real article, a real video/audio clip, a real photo for Describe a Picture); everything else is self-contained by default, including the ones that originally implied a real image or chart but don't strictly need one — see the ⚑ notes below for how those are generated instead. See SKILL.md's "Choosing the activities" section for the cap on how many externally-sourced activities a single lesson may include.

## Vocabulary & input
- **Fresh Vocabulary related to interests – focus on collocations**: the core vocab-presentation activity; see the Fresh Vocabulary section rule in SKILL.md — always collocation-first, tied to the student's interests. Self-contained.
- **Read a (generated) dialogue using new vocabulary**: write a short dialogue (2 speakers, 8-15 lines) embedding the lesson's new vocab/grammar naturally in context; good comprehension check before production. Self-contained.
- **⚑ Read a found article – discuss vocab, topic**: use or reference a real article on the student's interest area; pre-teach blocking vocab, discuss content and any new vocab encountered. Suits B1+ where authentic text is manageable. **Requires the tutor to source a real article** — there's no generated substitute, since the point is authentic native text.
- **⚑ Describe a picture**: student describes a real image in detail (or compares two images). **Requires the tutor to bring an actual photo** — a written description defeats the point of the task, since the student is meant to do the visual observation themselves, not read someone else's description of it. Add a Teacher's Note suggesting what kind of photo to look for (e.g. "a busy street market scene" or "a candid photo with several people doing different things"), tied to the lesson's theme. List the photo in Materials needed as something the tutor still needs to bring. Good low-prep speaking warm-up or vocab-activation task, scales from A2 (simple description) to C1 (inference, speculation about context)
- **⚑ Use a very short media clip, discuss message and opinions**: short video/audio clip (1-3 min) as a prompt for listening + opinion discussion; keep clip length short relative to discussion time. **Requires the tutor to source a real clip** — there's no generated substitute for authentic audio/video.
- **Use data or charts to discuss trends and solutions**: practice describing trends (rise/fall/plateau language) then discuss causes/solutions. Self-contained by default — generate a simple text-formatted data table or a described trend ("sales rose steadily from Jan-Mar, then dropped sharply in April") rather than requiring a real chart image. Good for B1+ and business-English contexts

## Speaking & discussion
- **Speaking Practice – Discussion topics and questions**: a set of open discussion questions on the lesson topic, ordered from concrete to abstract/opinion-based. Self-contained.
- **Sentence upgrades**: give a short list of several (4-6) plain/basic sentences tied to the lesson topic; student rewrites each one, applying one or more of the styles below. Not a single sentence rewritten multiple ways, the point is repeated practice across several sentences. Self-contained.
  - Using new vocab from this lesson
  - Changing the tone (funny, rude, formal, etc.)
  - Shifting between indirect and direct language (e.g. "Give me that" → "Would you mind passing that?")
- **Role Play**: assign roles and a scenario tied to the student's interest/goal (e.g. a workplace negotiation for a business student); give brief role cards, not full scripts. Self-contained.
- **Ranking scenario (4-5 items)**: give 4-5 items/options related to the topic; student ranks them by a criterion and justifies the order — strong for comparative language and opinion-giving. Self-contained.
- **Impossible choice scenario**: present a forced dilemma with no clearly "correct" answer; student must choose and defend it — good for hedging language, opinion structures. Self-contained.
- **Mini Debates**: short (5-10 min) debate on a topic connected to the lesson; assign or let student pick a side. Self-contained.
- **Devil's Advocate – argue for a different position**: after the student states an honest opinion, have them argue the opposite position; builds argumentation language and flexibility, best for B2+. Self-contained.
- **Inbox activity – respond to a series of messages (verbally or in writing)**: present 3-5 short generated messages (emails/texts) needing replies; student responds to each, either aloud or in writing — good for register-switching practice. Self-contained.
- **Press conference – respond to journalist questions**: student takes on a role (public figure, expert, someone from a scenario) and fields a rapid series of generated questions; strong for fluency under mild pressure, works well for exam-interview prep. Self-contained.

## Roleplay/scenario formats needing setup detail
- **Gallery tour – describe a series of artworks and respond to questions**: present 3-4 pieces. Self-contained by default — write short vivid descriptions of invented (or well-known, described-not-shown) artworks/visuals for the student to "guide a tour" through, rather than requiring actual images; only use real images if the tutor supplies them. Good extended-speaking task.
- **Respond to Subway takes with a randomly selected tone**: present a short generated opinionated "hot take" style statement (in the vein of social-media "unpopular opinion" posts); student responds, using a tone assigned at random (e.g. diplomatic, sarcastic, enthusiastic) — good register/tone-flexibility practice, best B1+. Self-contained.
- **Hometown Hero local newspaper interview**: student plays someone being interviewed as a "local hero" for a hometown newspaper; interviewer (tutor or peer) asks human-interest questions — combines narrative past tenses with personalization if tied to the student's actual background. Self-contained.

## Fit notes
- Debate/Devil's Advocate/Press conference need enough language for argumentation — reserve for B1+ (ideally B2+)
- Describe a picture, Ranking scenario, and Role Play scale down cleanly to A2 with simpler scenarios/vocab
- The three ⚑ activities (found article, media clip, describe a picture) are the only ones that inherently depend on the tutor sourcing real material; everything else generates fully from the lesson's content
- Don't default to complex native-speed content for A1-A2, whether generated or authentic

## Group classroom notes

The entries above were written for one student. In english-classroom, every activity runs with a class, so apply these adjustments. Write each activity as the Core version, then add Support and Stretch versions (see `differentiation.md`) and a suggested grouping.

- **Works in pairs, trios, or small groups as written**: Read a (generated) dialogue, Speaking Practice, Role Play (roles in pairs or trios, with an observer in trios), Ranking scenario (rank individually, then agree a group ranking), Impossible choice, Mini Debates (two teams), Inbox activity (reply, then compare with a partner), Gallery tour (groups circulate), Hometown Hero (interviewer and interviewee pairs, then swap), Respond to Subway takes (pass the take around the group).
- **Needs adapting**: Devil's Advocate (pairs: one states an opinion, the partner argues against, then swap), Press conference (running it with one student on stage at a time is slow for a class, so use small groups where one student answers and the others ask, rotating the role), Sentence upgrades (work individually, then compare in pairs).
- **Performance-style activities**: any activity where one student speaks alone in front of the whole room should appear at most once per lesson, and the teacher should rotate who goes first across lessons.
- **Describe a picture**: pairs describe a photo to each other. It still counts against the one external-material activity per lesson. The teacher can project one photo or give each pair a different one.
- **Found article, short media clip, data or charts**: everyone works from the same material (projected, or one copy per pair), then discusses in small groups and reports back. A real article or clip counts against the one external-material activity per lesson; generated data does not.
- **Fresh vocabulary**: one list for the whole class, with scaffolds in the Support tier. See `differentiation.md`.
